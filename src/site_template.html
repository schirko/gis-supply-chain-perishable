Key findings

  • Demand follows a weekly rhythm. The day of the week and the last four weeks' average matter more than anything else the model was given. Even the simple rules (repeat last week, or take the median of recent weeks) miss by only ${wape_median8}% to ${wape_naive}% on the test days, which is the bar the model had to beat.
  • Gradient boosting beats the simple rules by about ${improvement}%. Scored once on the last ${test_days} days of the data (${test_window}), it misses by ${wape_model}% on average. The error is measured as total absolute miss divided by total units sold (WAPE).
  • It leans slightly high, not low. Over the test days the forecasts total ${bias}% more than what sold, a small tilt toward overstock. The median-of-recent-weeks rule leans further high (+${bias_median8}%), the seasonal-naive rule +${bias_naive}%.
  • Smaller regions are harder to forecast. Misses by warehouse: ${wape_by_wh}. Fewer stores means more noise.

How it was done

  1. Audited the data. Checked the Kaggle store-sales files for gaps, zero-sales days and what a sale actually represents.
  2. Put the stores on a map. Looked up each store city, checked every point against its province, fixed the ones that were wrong, and grouped stores into four assumed warehouse regions.
  3. Chose the first product family. Compared the perishable families by how often a warehouse sells nothing, and picked bread and bakery to build first.
  4. Forecast it. Split the history by time (never shuffled), set simple baselines first, tried a linear model and two gradient boosting versions, picked the winner on validation, and scored the test period one time.

Limits. The warehouse sites are assumed, not Favorita's real network. Sales are not true demand, because a sold-out shelf hides what customers wanted. Forecasts are one day ahead and use actual sales from the previous days. The test weeks do not include Mother's Day or May 1 (the two hardest days to forecast in validation) or the year-end season. Only bread and bakery is covered so far; the other perishable families are not yet forecast. No forecast ranges yet. The stocking recommendation and the interactive app are the next milestones.
